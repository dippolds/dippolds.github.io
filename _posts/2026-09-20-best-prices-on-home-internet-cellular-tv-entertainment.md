People often ask me to explain how I pay $25 for cellular, $60 for home internet, $30 for TV. It is not that simple but I wanted to write it down.

## Service Defintions and what it should cost
Cellular (or Mobile) service - this is probably the one thing you probably need as a modern human. This is not the phone device but the services that hooks you up to the world for voice, texting, and the internet. Expect to pay $15 to $60 per month.

Home internet - sometimes people this WiFi but this is a connection from your home to the world. Not required if you have cell services on a phone but most people like the speed and capacity. Expect to pay $40 to $100 a month. 

TV entertainment - Some people will call this cable and streaming. Again not required and can be free with an antenna or online services like Youtube. This is content and there is no one package that gives you all content. Pay $0 to $300+ per month. 

Home dial-up voice - largely gone these days as most people people use their cellphone. There are free options for the tech-savy. Try to avoid but may be needed places that don't have cellular service. $0 to $60+. 

## How companies complicate things to get more money out of you

* Bundling the services together. This almost never works out for the consumer as you are paying for things you don't use. Before agreeing to a bundle, itemize what you want.
* Introductory offer - Often companies will automatically increase the price after 1 year. Or if you try to get out of a long-contract, there is a penalty. Check both aspects before being tempted by an introductory offer.
* Discount on a phone or router - It is almost never a good finanical decision to rent or get a free phone from a carrier. They are simply charging you a lot more per month and after paying for your phone, reaping in huge amounts later. Also, carriers will have phones locked to them so you can't switch carriers without getting them to unlock the phone. Simply buy the phone. If you can't afford the phone, your are simply getting a phone that is too expensive for your income or not managing your money. 
* Personal service is a cost and choice - If you want to walk into a store and have someone help you, that is a cost for the company. So what you pay will be higher. If you want to pay less, you take on the risk and hassle yourself. 
* Internet speed - Almost nobody needs more than 50 Mbps and most basic plans are greater than 200 Mbps. The con is oh you have 3 people in your house, you need 1000 Mbps. Get the lowest price for 50 or greater. Note is some very rural places 50 Mbps is a challenge.

## Suggestions

   1. Write down what you want in the 3 categories. Example I want an iPhone and I use Spotify to stream music in my car 1 hour per day. I want wifi for my laptop at home and to watch Netflix and local news. I also want someone to help me in person, not just over the phone.
   2. Ask people who live you what internet providers there are. Verizon, Xfinity for example provide wired connections to houses. Tmobile may provide a connection to your house via wireless (5g) which may be cost effective if you are not sreaming many hours per day. Satelite is also an option but will tend to be more costly. Look-up online what each company costs. Shoot for $40-$60.
   3. Ask people what cell companies they use. There are really 2 types of companies to consider. Verizon, T-Mobile, and AT&T are the top tier, run the networks and have stores. Expect to pay more (shoot for $50/month) for these carriers but choose one that has a store and people who live in your area says has good cell signal. The lower cost companies basically rent the signal from the major carriers so most of the time the actualy service is the same. Example Mint Mobile uses T-Mobile (and is actually owned by T-Mobile). Mint will cost about $15-$25 per month but any help is over the phone. No store. 
   4. TV entertainment is largely streaming (meaning you pick what to watch on-demand rather than flipping channels). If you want your local network channels (i.e. like cable), there are 2 choices; a set-top box like traditional cable and streaming through the same device as services like Netflix. For example, Sling TV starts at $30 per month for a bunch of channels. However, if you only cared about the Hallmark channel, you can pay for Hallmark+ for $9 a month. 
   5. Devices - There are 3 main devices you need. A cell phone (make sure it is unlocked when you buy it, a streaming box (like Roku or Apple TV or what is built into a modern TV), and a router (gives your house a wifi signal). Costs are $250-$1000, $75 and $75 respectively. Buy and don't rent!
