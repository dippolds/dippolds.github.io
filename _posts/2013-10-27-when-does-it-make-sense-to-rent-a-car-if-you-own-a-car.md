Are there good reasons to rent a car when you own a car already? In short, it depends.![20131027-191608.jpg](/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/20131027-191608.jpg) **1. Being somewhere your car isn't** (travel) **- <span style="text-decoration: underline;">Yes</span>**  **2. Car in the shop** (or know something is wrong with your car) **- <span style="text-decoration: underline;">Yes </span>** **3. Car isn't big enough** for hauling people or stuff - <span style="text-decoration: underline;">**Yes**</span> Makes sense. In fact, clever people will buy a small car that meets 90%+ of their needs and rent on occcasion. Many people can occasionally make good use of a pickup truck. However, paying a premium for a truck, insurance, and poorer gas milage will be more than buying a small car and renting a truck on occasion. **4. Car failure rate based on age** - <span style="text-decoration: underline;">**Maybe**</span> The[ BBC found](http://www.bbc.co.uk/blogs/opensecrets/2010/01/mot_failure_rates_released.html) that during the course of a year, a 3 year old car will has a 15-30% failure rate while a 10 year old car will have a 30-50% failure rate. But this is on vehicle inspection, not saying the car is broken down leaving one stranded. Our intuition says older cars break down more frequently but know of many stories new cars being lemons while some old cars going on forever. Unfortunately, it is very difficult to predict that choosing a rental car will be more reliable. There is some solice that if you are somewhere and the rental car breaks, it is the rental car companies burden but you will be kicking yourself for your own car. **5. Range of electric car** - **<span style="text-decoration: underline;">Yes</span>** If your main car is an electric car, it is likely to be unreasonable electric for more than 200 miles a day.
