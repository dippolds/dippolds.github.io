### Challenge

<div></div><div>All of those beautiful pieces of wood that don't stack well can be quickly made into an tablet or smartphone stand.</div><div></div><div></div><div></div><div></div><div>[![](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1150026571.jpg)](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1150026571.jpg)</div>### Technique

 My great lumberjack father provided some nice burl pieces. However, it was a struggle to find the right workshop tool to cut these irregular pieces. Finally, the obvious occurred; use a chain saw to cut the wood. <div></div><div>[![](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1515694397.jpg)](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1515694397.jpg)</div><div></div><div>If they are small pieces, hold them down in a large vise like a Workmate.</div><div></div><div>[![](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1695402027.jpg)](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1695402027.jpg)</div><div></div><div>Ideally, shape the parts on the uncut logs as the logs are the perfect vise.</div><div></div><div>[![](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1817707257.jpg)](/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/blogger-image-1817707257.jpg)</div><div></div><div><span>It can be a little difficult to get the cuts the right depth and parallel to the base. Since it only takes 2 minutes to make one, make ten and pick the best. Note the void in some trees like above even avoids the need to drill a hole for a charging cable.</span></div></div>
